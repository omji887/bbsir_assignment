Model, limitations & equations

This is a transparent teaching model, not calibrated high-altitude network planning. One message is an 8-kilobit equivalent unit; it does not equate satellite voice, radio voice and mobile data bandwidth in real operations. Capacities are normalized to essential-message throughput.

  • The region is a notional 100 km². Digital/radio coverage = min(1, site count × π × effective range² × 0.55 / 100). The 0.55 factor represents terrain obstruction. Satellite coverage is a sky-view setting, independent of tower density.
  • Power controls are active watts per handset or terminal. Effective terrestrial range scales with sqrt(power / reference power). A fixed 240 Wh battery per endpoint, a 10% active duty cycle and a 20 W grid allocation per endpoint are assumed. Once grid power is lost, energy use accumulates and battery depletion reduces availability. Mobile sites consume 700 W per tower, radio sites 80 W per antenna, satellite ground sites 20 W per antenna; these are illustrative fixed loads.
  • Capacity is the minimum of equivalent bandwidth / 8 kbit, antenna processing capacity, and endpoint/operator capacity, multiplied by infrastructure health and coverage. Digital antennas, mobile towers, radio repeater antennas, and satellite ground-terminal antennas are distinct controls.
  • Original demand = inventory × base traffic × disaster surge (surge starts after grace time). Locally reachable demand is offered first. Unreachable demand is optionally distributed to other live networks, proportional to their capacities; no second redistribution loop occurs. Capacity overflow remains unserved. The relay toggle assumes an agency can relay messages for a community member, not that an ordinary mobile can use a satellite or radio link.
  • After the normal-operation grace period, each network’s ground equipment degrades linearly to zero at the damage times shown by each scenario. Weather attenuation is separate and recoverable when intensity changes. Damage and used battery energy do not reverse as time advances; reset restores them.
  • Disaster intensities and timing profiles are hypothetical. Real failure order depends on siting, protection, redundancy, backup power, spectrum, line of sight and operator availability. Satellite failures mean ground terminals / antennas, not satellites in orbit.
  • Satellite is modeled as weather/sky-view limited, not circular ground range. Its slider shows nominal footprint radius for context; actual availability is controlled by sky visibility. A tsunami is generally not a direct hazard to inland high-altitude terrain; this option is a deliberately hypothetical inundation scenario.
